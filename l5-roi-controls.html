The honest headline number
Recoverable cash is R$199.52 — not R$271,891.01. Most of the exposure is a refund liability (CANCELED_WITH_PAYMENT) or a data-integrity gap (MISSING_ITEMS), not cash walking in the door. The real business case for this workflow is faster, more consistent resolution of that exposure — not cash recovery.

Why 0.31 isn't the number to lead with in an interview
The 1,618 exceptions are a multi-year historical backlog (2016–2018 order data), not a monthly volume. "0.31 cycles to break even" describes clearing that backlog once — it says nothing about ongoing monthly ROI, which would need an actual monthly exception volume to compute honestly. Presented without this caveat, the number overstates the case; presented with it, it's a defensible one-time efficiency gain plus an open question for further analysis.

03
Business analyst readiness questions
What I'd need answered before recommending this be built for a real organization — not built yet, deliberately
This section is explicitly hypothetical: a checklist of what a BA should ask before scoping this for a real company, not a claim that these questions were asked or answered here. The point is the instinct to ask business questions before technical ones — not the specific answers, which don't exist for a synthetic case study.
Questions I'd ask before scoping this for real
Which ERP or order-management system is the source of truth today, and does it expose an API?
Who currently has approval authority over a refund or write-off, and at what dollar threshold does that authority change?
Is there an existing SLA for exception resolution, and what does "resolved" mean today?
Which exception types legally or contractually require escalation beyond finance (e.g. to legal, or to the customer directly)?
Where do audit logs live today, and who has access to them?
Which KPIs does finance leadership actually review weekly — exposure dollars, case age, or resolution rate?
What regulatory regime applies (SOX, LGPD given this is Brazilian data, PCI if payment data is involved)?
What's the organization's acceptable AI risk tolerance — is a drafting-only AI acceptable, or is even that too much for this workflow?
