Inside the spike · Engineering, July 2026

Daily cost on the 11 flagged days vs a trailing median of ≈ $37/day. Detector rule: daily cost > 3× trailing 28-day median.

An agent experiment at 3.5× normal volume on the priciest model. The fix isn't “stop experimenting” — it's a per-experiment budget key and this alert running daily, not at month-end.

FINDING 01

One experiment cost $2,384 before anyone noticed

Engineering's July spend hit $3,219 — 268% of cap and ~3.7× a normal month. Eleven flagged days, all one experiment. A daily spike alert would have caught it on day one.

FINDING 02

The premium model is doing routine work: ~$812 avoidable

10,500 calls under 3,000 input tokens ran on the top-tier model at $1,015; priced at mid-tier they'd cost $203. Routing small calls down a tier saves ~$135/month, no workflow change.

FINDING 03

Support will breach its cap next month — from growth

Support closed September at 96.3% of its $340 cap with cost per ticket flat at $0.028. That's healthy adoption hitting a static budget: review the cap, don't freeze the spend. Failed/retried calls also burned $382 over 6 months.

About this prototype. Animated web version of the Token Analytics project (DuckDB + SQL, 5 models: cost-per-call ledger, daily cost by team, cost per ticket resolved, spike flags, budget burn). Every number on this page comes from the project's actual output files — 540,431 synthetic LLM calls for a fictional ~74-person company, Apr–Sep 2026, priced at published list rates for 5 models. Allocation assumption, stated plainly: a team's monthly AI spend is allocated across the tickets it resolved that month. Built as a design prototype alongside the planned Power BI one-pager.
